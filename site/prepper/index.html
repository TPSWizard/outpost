What's inside

Built for the first 72 hours
and the next 90 days.

Every feature is designed to work with the phone in airplane mode, the screen dimmed, and the battery on a budget.

Today

Start the outage counter and get a daily checklist for water, food, health, security, communication, and morale. It changes by phase (first 72 hours, settling in, long haul) and adapts to pets, infants, medications, and climate.

Offline maps

Download your region once. GPS, pins, rally points, and a printable neighborhood map, with no cell service.

Offline library

Wikipedia, medical encyclopedias, water treatment, repair guides, and gardening Q&A as Kiwix libraries with full-text search.

First aid

Yes/no checklists for bleeding, burns, fractures, heat, cold, dehydration, and infection. Every step cites the Army manual page and opens it.

Supplies

Water, food, fuel, batteries, medications. See how many days each lasts for your household, with warnings under two weeks.

Power

Survival mode: red-on-black, 60 Hz, background work paused. A planner turns solar, power bank, and car charging into a daily budget.

AI assistant

Runs on the phone, no internet. Answers from your libraries and manuals, cites the page, and asks before it changes anything.

Radio & sharing

FRS/GMRS, MURS, CB, NOAA, and ham frequencies, a log, and phone-to-phone sharing over Bluetooth. Meshtastic support, experimental.

Paper backup

Print the family plan, medication schedule, water and first-aid quick reference, and an "if this phone dies" summary.

Allow & install

If Android says apps from this source aren't allowed, tap Settings, allow it, go back, then Install.

Prepare

Open Today → Before the outage. It lists the maps, libraries, manuals, and AI model to download now, and checks them off as you go.

Why the warning? Outpost installs directly rather than from the Play Store, so Android shows a notice about unknown sources. That's expected. Updates install the same way, on top of the old version, and your data stays.

Privacy

Your data never leaves the phone.

  • Household, supplies, notes, and pins stay on the phone or your SD card.
  • The internet is used only for downloads you start.
  • Open source under the GPL v3. Read the code, build it yourself.
  • Medical tools follow published protocols and are not a substitute for a clinician.
